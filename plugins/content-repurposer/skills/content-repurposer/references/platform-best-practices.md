# Platform Best Practices

Last reviewed: October 2026. Platforms change their rules often; update this file
when you notice something has changed.

---

## LinkedIn

**How reach works**
- LinkedIn rewards *dwell time* (how long people spend reading) more than quick likes.
- Early engagement matters: comments in the first hour help the post reach more people.
- Posts with external links get noticeably less reach. Putting the link in the first
  comment is also reported to be penalized now, so share the value natively.

**Formats, strongest to weakest**
1. Document / PDF carousels (5–10 slides, 1080 × 1350 or 1080 × 1080)
2. Native video (30–90 seconds, with captions, uploaded directly)
3. Image + text
4. Text only
- Polls currently perform poorly; avoid them.

**Writing do's**
- Open with a specific, concrete first line. Bad: "Excited to share some thoughts on
  leadership." Good: "I lost my best engineer because of one meeting I skipped."
- Keep paragraphs to 1–3 lines with white space between them.
- Use first person and share a real lesson, mistake, or result.
- Use numbers and specifics ("cut onboarding from 3 weeks to 4 days").
- End with a real question that people can answer from experience.

**Writing don'ts**
- No engagement bait ("Comment YES", "Agree? 👇", "Like if…").
- No hashtag walls; 0–2 relevant hashtags at most.
- No corporate buzzwords or generic motivation.
- Don't tag many people who aren't involved.

**Posting habits (tell the user if useful)**
- Reply to comments within the first hour.
- One valuable post a week beats five forgettable ones; personal profiles can post
  daily if quality stays high.

## Instagram

**How reach works**
- Carousels earn extra time on the post because each swipe counts, and Instagram may
  show the carousel again starting from slide 2 to people who skipped it.
- Saves and shares (sends) are strong signals; write content worth saving.
- Instagram now limits posts to **5 hashtags**. Use 3–5 targeted ones.
- Instagram search reads captions, so plain keywords help people find the post.

**Carousel do's**
- Slide 1: a bold, specific hook ("5 mistakes killing your resume"). Make it readable
  in under 2 seconds.
- Slide 2: deliver value immediately, so people who see it first still get hooked.
- One idea per slide; big, readable text; consistent colors and fonts.
- Make the slides flow as one story; each slide should make people want the next.
- Last slide: a clear call to action (save, share, follow, comment).
- Size: 1080 × 1350 px (4:5 portrait) fills the most screen space.

**Caption do's**
- First line: a hook or a question, because the rest is hidden behind "more".
- Add context or a short story the slides don't cover.
- Use line breaks for readability; emojis only as light accents.
- End with one clear call to action.

**Don'ts**
- Don't paste the LinkedIn post as the caption.
- Don't put links in captions (they aren't clickable); say "link in bio" if needed.
- Don't use generic hashtags like #love or #instagood.

**Reels (if the user asks)**
- Hook in the first 1–2 seconds, add on-screen text and captions, and keep it short.

## X (Twitter)

**How reach works**
- Replies are the strongest engagement signal, followed by reposts and profile clicks;
  likes and bookmarks count for less.
- Posts with external links get less reach. Post the message first and put the link
  in the first reply.
- Recent posts get a boost, and posting consistently in one niche helps the right
  people find you.
- Native video (under ~2 minutes) and images outperform plain text. Upload videos
  directly instead of linking YouTube.
- Many mutes or blocks reduce overall reach.

**Writing do's**
- Lead with a specific claim, surprising fact, or useful question people can react to.
- Short sentences; cut every unnecessary word.
- Threads: post 1 must stand alone and promise value ("How I grew X in 30 days 🧵").
  One idea per post. End by asking for replies or opinions.
- Polls work well for sparking replies.

**Don'ts**
- No external link in the main post or the first post of a thread.
- No hashtag stuffing; 0–1 at most.
- No generic, AI-sounding filler. Premium users can now flag replies as low quality
  or AI-generated, so write with a clear point of view.

**Posting habits (tell the user if useful)**
- 3–5 well-spaced posts a day is plenty; more doesn't multiply reach.
- Reply to people in your niche; posts from mutual follows get more visibility.

## Cross-platform rules
- Same core message, different packaging: LinkedIn = lesson, Instagram = visual
  guide, X = sharp take.
- Never invent statistics, quotes, or results that aren't in the source.
- Write for one specific reader, not "everyone".
- Accessibility: suggest alt text for images and captions for videos.
