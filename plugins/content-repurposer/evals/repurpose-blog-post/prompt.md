Why I Stopped Checking Email First Thing in the Morning

For five years I opened my inbox the moment I woke up. By 9 a.m. I was already reacting to other people's priorities. Three months ago I tried an experiment: no email until 11 a.m. Instead, I spent the first two hours on my most important project.

The results surprised me. I finished a book draft I had been stuck on for a year. My stress dropped, because I was no longer starting the day in catch-up mode. And nobody complained: only two emails in three months truly needed a reply before 11.

Three things made it work: I told my team about the change, I turned off phone notifications, and I wrote down my one important task the night before. If you try this, start with just one hour.
